서강젊은광고인네트워크

Hello.

Maybe.......... Do you happen to love advertising?

It doesn’t have to be advertising.
Design, marketing, branding — any of it.

If you’ve ever stopped twice at a subway billboard,
felt good all day because of one well-made logo,
or stared a little too long at a favorite brand’s new packaging,
we’re probably alike.

I made this invitation for fellow Sogang students like that.

Lately, I often think about this.

AI is changing the world day by day,
yet the way college students dreaming of advertising and marketing build experience
still feels much the same as before.

Everyone walks a similar path in a similar way,
and it’s hard to show your own color there.
Above all, it doesn’t make your heart race.

However hard you work as a student supporter,
it ends with a certificate.
Ad pitches you stayed up all night for in an intercollegiate club
still have a long way to go before becoming real campaigns.
And contest ideas win a prize
and go straight into a drawer.

But we can’t just stop.
There must be another way —
a way to do communication that truly talks to the world.

I’m still a student too, and I’ve walked that same road.

I joined student supporter programs,
did a bit of research,
and made public-service posters and put them up myself.

Then, at a startup competition I happened to enter, I won a Minister’s Award,
and that led me to join a political marketing startup.

I pitched directly to members of the National Assembly,
and an OOH campaign I made hit peak search volume,
leading to coverage across many news outlets.

Running content marketing, I shot the videos myself,
passed 9 million total views on our own SNS channels,
and wrote more proposals than I can count.

I left to do more of it with my own hands,
and now run a small ad agency while still in school.

We shoot SNS content with influencers and create events together.
I’ve created a character for a coffee brand,
designed logos and brand identities,
and photographed events on site in Gangnam.

An outdoor campaign in Seongsu was featured in magazines,
and this winter our team’s ads go up
in Gangnam subway stations and on buses.

I’m not trying to show off my record.
I just wanted to share this passion for talking to the world
with people who feel the same.

SYAN is a network of those people.

People who want to surprise the world with something they made.
People who want to win at international ad festivals.
People who want to do it all themselves, A to Z, from planning to launch.

Sparks don’t fly alone.
The moment people with the same passion collide,
real ideas leap out.
I believe that small spark can change the world a little.

SYAN isn’t a club, or an academic society with a fixed schedule.
Think of it simply as a network.

If it needs a name, it’s closest to the original meaning of “extracurricular activity.”

In Korean, 對外活動.
Facing the world outside, and moving with life.

Stepping off campus, meeting the real world,
and testing your own ideas.
SYAN wants to bring that meaning back.

I know everyone already has busy projects.
Rather than adding more to your plate,
I hope this becomes the plus-alpha to your projects —
a place that amplifies your thinking one step further.

Advertising, design, marketing, branding.
Share good work you’ve seen or an idea that stuck with you,
and when something good comes out,
we move on it together.

I’m still learning, but I want to help however I can.

Talks with people who’ve won at international ad festivals.
Putting projects of our own, like public-service ads, out into the world.
Connecting you with the clients and founders I meet
for meetups and coffee chats.

What I have now, and what I’ll build from here —
I want to connect as much of it with you as I can.

You don’t have to be finished yet.
We’re still students, after all.
I hope many people better than me will join.
I’ll learn a lot, and share a lot.

It would be an honor
to sketch the first draft of your advertising career together.

You don’t need a grand or impressive story. Just tell me yours.
I'll read every one myself, carefully.
